# Proposed Kosovo pilot

**Hypothesis.** A university registrar can save staff time on routine authenticity checks if employers can verify a graduate-authorized link themselves. The university registrar's office would sponsor a pilot, institutional IT would operate it, graduates would choose what to share, and HR teams would check the proof. There are no measured savings, confirmed partners or commitments to purchase.

| Pilot stage | What to learn | Evidence to collect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Interview two registrar staff and two employer representatives about verification requests, consent and exceptions. | Anonymized baseline request count, staff minutes, turnaround time and decision authority. |
| Week 2 | Registrar and IT approve a synthetic cohort, disclosure policy and support path. | Approval record, setup time, role and proof checks. |
| Week 3 | Run moderated graduate and employer tasks using synthetic diplomas. | Share completion, median verification time, failure reasons and whether the employer correctly describes the result. |
| Week 4 | Compare the pilot with the baseline and review support and governance costs. | Counts and denominators, observed limitations, institution's decision to continue or stop. |

Initial recruitment would go through registrar offices and career services. Request introductions to employers who already verify qualifications. Institutional procurement, legal review, accreditation checks, real identity provisioning and a durable chain trust policy precede any use with real diplomas. A local chain proves the prototype's technical workflow. It does not establish an institution's legal authority.
