# Proposed Kosovo pilot

**Hypothesis.** A university registrar can save staff time on routine authenticity checks if employers can verify a graduate-authorized link themselves. The university registrar's office would sponsor a pilot, institutional IT would operate it, graduates would choose what to share, and HR teams would check the proof. There are no measured savings, confirmed partners or commitments to purchase.

| Pilot stage | What to learn | Evidence to collect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Interview two registrar staff and two employer representatives about verification requests, consent and exceptions. | Anonymized baseline request count, staff minutes, turnaround time and decision authority. |
| Week 2 | Registrar and IT approve a synthetic cohort, disclosure policy and support path. | Approval record, setup time, role and proof checks. |
| Week 3 | Run moderated graduate and employer tasks using synthetic diplomas. | Share completion, median verification time, failure reasons and whether the employer correctly describes the result. |
| Week 4 | Compare the pilot with the baseline and review support and governance costs. | Counts and denominators, observed limitations, institution's decision to continue or stop. |

Initial recruitment would go through registrar offices and career services. Request introductions to employers who already verify qualifications. Institutional procurement, legal review, accreditation checks, real identity provisioning and a durable chain trust policy precede any use with real diplomas. A local chain proves the prototype's technical workflow. It does not establish an institution's legal authority.

## Pilot decision and measurement

The proposed first use case is an employer checking a qualification during recruitment with the graduate's permission. Ask a registrar to walk through one recent routine request from receipt to reply and one exception requiring investigation. Ask an employer what evidence they would accept, who can authorize that decision, and when they would still contact the university.

| Measure | How the proposed pilot would collect it |
| --- | --- |
| Staff effort | Time active registrar work separately from elapsed time waiting for a reply. Compare median staff minutes per completed routine check across the existing process and the synthetic pilot. Record the number of observations for each. |
| Self-service completion | Count verifier tasks completed with the correct decision and without registrar help, divided by all assigned verifier tasks. Record expired links, unavailable evidence and requests for help separately. A correct refusal to accept an expired share counts as a correct decision. |
| Understanding and privacy | After each task, ask what the result established and which information was disclosed. Record whether the participant distinguished record integrity from accreditation and presenter identity. Keep aggregate counts and anonymized observations. |

At the end of week four, the registrar sponsor and institutional IT would review these measures alongside setup time, support workload and unresolved exceptions. Their decision would be to continue synthetic evaluation, revise the workflow or stop. Any move to real records requires separate institutional approval. Interviews, timings and buyer commitments are still pending.
